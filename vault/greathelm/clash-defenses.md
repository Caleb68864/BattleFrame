# Clash Defenses — Four Choices, Declared Blind

QSR p2, verbatim:

> The defender must choose one of the following **before rolling** a clash test:
>
> **Parry:** Success defends all damage and grants the defender an additional momentum.
> **Riposte:** Success defends all damage and deals attacker 1 damage with no armor table roll.
> **Block.** Defenders with shields gain +2 to this defense. Success defends all damage.
> **Dodge.** Success defends all damage and grants the defender 1" of movement to use immediately.

## Comparison

All four "defend all damage" on success — they are identical in defensive outcome. They differ **only in the rider**:

| Defense | Rider on success | Requires |
|---|---|---|
| Parry | +1 momentum (so **+2 total** — one for winning the clash, one from Parry) | — |
| Riposte | Deal attacker 1 damage, **bypassing the armor table** | — |
| Block | Nothing — but **+2 to the roll** with a shield | Shield (for the bonus) |
| Dodge | 1" immediate move | — |

So the choice is: *win harder* (Block) vs *punish* (Riposte) vs *bank resource* (Parry) vs *reposition* (Dodge).

**Riposte is the standout** — it is the only effect in the game that deals damage with "no armor table roll", i.e. guaranteed damage. See [[heavy-armor-table]]. It is also the only way a defender deals damage at all.

Only **Block** has a bonus; the other three are unmodified d6. So Riposte/Parry/Dodge are strictly riskier than Block for a shielded knight — you trade win probability for the rider.

## Declared before rolling

This is the blind-commitment point. Forgetting it triggers [[hasty-rolling]].

## Not found

Whether a defense can be chosen while *not* in base contact (i.e. vs ranged), and whether Dodge's 1" can leave base contact. Goonhammer confirms "Dodging allows you to move up to 1"" without adding constraints.
