# Initiative Order Determination (Most 6s)

Who acts first is decided by **counting 6s**, not by a separate initiative roll.

QSR p1, verbatim:

> The player with the most 6s chooses whether to go first or second this round. If neither player has 6s, look at 5s, then 4s, and so on. If only one player has 6s, that player must go first.

## Three distinct cases

1. **Most 6s** → that player *chooses* first or second. It is a choice, not an obligation.
2. **Neither player has 6s** → compare 5s, then 4s, and so on down.
3. **Only one player has 6s** → that player **must** go first. No choice.

Case 3 is a genuine trap and easy to miss: holding the sole 6 *removes* your choice and forces you to commit first. Going first is not unambiguously good here, since [[battle-phase-initiative-steps]] alternation means acting first also means revealing intent first.

## Unresolved: exact ties

If both players have equal counts at every face, the QSR gives no tiebreak — **not found**. Goonhammer offers only: "if you both roll the exact same dice then congrats, just roll off or something" (https://www.tabletopbattles.com/goonhammer-reviews-greathelm-a-micro-skirmish-game-of-chivalric-fantasy/) — which reads as the reviewer improvising, not quoting a rule.

## Source conflict (resolved)

Goonhammer says "the player with the most 6's wins initiative and activates a model first" — i.e. winner *acts* first. The QSR and the Kickstarter both say the winner *decides*. The QSR is authoritative; Goonhammer is simplifying. See [[version-discrepancies-qsr-vs-kickstarter]].
