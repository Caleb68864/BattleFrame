# Games Workshop IP is radioactive — and explicitly bans apps

Disposes of the entire Mordheim/Necromunda community-ruleset cluster. GW's IP guidelines don't merely fail to grant rights — they **name software specifically** as prohibited.

## The killer clause

GW's Intellectual Property Guidelines state, verbatim:

> "Individuals must not create computer games or apps based on our characters and settings. These are only to be created under licence from Games Workshop."

This is not a general copyright statement that a module might arguably slip past. It is a **specific, explicit prohibition on exactly the category of thing this project builds.** There is no reading of this under which a GW-based Foundry module is permitted fan content.

## The rest of the policy

> "Non-commercial, with no money being received or paid. This includes all forms of fundraising activity, and generation of any advertising revenue."

> "Games Workshop retains ownership in respect of the underlying intellectual property rights in those worlds, and therefore such fan content cannot be sold or otherwise monetised."

> "These guidelines do not constitute formal approval or authorization of any fan content, or a waiver of our legal rights."

That last sentence is the important one. Even compliant fan content receives **no protection whatsoever** — GW reserves the right to act against it regardless.

The quotes above come from [40kfan.com](https://40kfan.com/games-workshop-intellectual-property-guidelines/), which reproduces the official guidelines, and are consistent across secondary sources. The **direction** is unambiguous and famously well-established. Human should read the primary page before any decision — but the decision here is "don't," which needs no further verification.

## Why this matters beyond GW

GW is the most litigious actor in this hobby, with a documented history of pursuing fan projects and a specific track record against **digital tools**. The asymmetry is total: no upside worth the risk.

Applies to: [[mordheim-community-rules]], [[necromunda-community-edition]], and any Warhammer/40K/Age of Sigmar-derived ruleset.

## The general lesson

Community rulesets built on someone else's IP inherit that IP's licence — which is **all rights reserved plus a revocable tolerance**. The community's work (NCE's rules patches, Mordheim's fan continuations) is real creative labour, but it is **legally built on sand**. The fan authors cannot grant rights they never had.

> A widely-circulated fan PDF is evidence of lax enforcement, not of permission. Ubiquity is not a licence.
