# Space Weirdos

Cheap, light, well-liked itch.io sci-fi skirmish. Paid and unlicensed — but the **community has already asked the author to open-license it**, which makes it a live prospect.

## Identity

- **Author:** Casey G.
- **URL:** https://caseyg.itch.io/space-weirdos

## Licensing — paid, no open licence found

**Confirmed as to absence.** Priced at **$5.00 USD or more**. No CC or OGL statement on the itch.io page. Standard copyright applies. **Cannot ship content.**

> [!note] The revealing detail
> A commenter on the page **explicitly asked the author to consider CC/OGL licensing** — which both confirms it currently isn't licensed, and shows the ask has already been raised by someone else. The author's response was not captured in this research (**unverified**).
>
> That's a genuinely useful signal. A $5 indie itch.io game, no publisher, no corporate owner, an author already fielding licensing questions — this is the **easiest permission ask in the survey**. Unlike [[five-parsecs-from-home]] (Modiphius owns it) or anything Osprey ([[osprey-games-blanket-copyright-policy]]), there's exactly one person to email and no commercial reason for them to say no to a free VTT module that drives $5 sales.
>
> **Recommended action: check the itch.io comment thread for the author's answer, then just ask.**

## Activation model — command points over alternating activation

**Partial** (search-derived from [Gundobad Games review](https://gundobadgames.blogspot.com/2021/09/review-space-weirdos-skirmish.html); not primary-fetched):

- Three phases: **Command Phase → Initiative Phase → Activation Phase**
- **Alternating individual-model activations** (explicitly **not** strict IGOUGO)
- A small **Command Point pool**, spendable for actions like overwatch shots

> [!tip] Command points are a resource layer over the scheduler
> The interesting bit isn't the alternating activation — it's the **CP pool sitting on top**, letting players spend a limited shared resource to act out of sequence (overwatch, reactions). That's a *second, orthogonal* scheduling mechanism layered over the first.
>
> Relevant because it's a common modern design and it breaks the "activation order fully determines who acts when" assumption — interrupts are purchasable. Cheap to support if anticipated; painful to retrofit.

## Complexity

**Light.** Small warbands, simple stats, quick play. A realistic scope for a first module.

## Suitability

**Best combination of tractable-ask + right-size in the survey.** Not currently licensed, so not shippable today — but it is light, cheap, popular, VTT-friendly, solo-author-owned, and the author is already thinking about licensing.

Ranks alongside [[forbidden-psalm]] and [[danger-close]] as a top prospect, differing in kind: Danger Close is *already* licensed, Forbidden Psalm sits in an *open ecosystem*, Space Weirdos needs *one email*.
