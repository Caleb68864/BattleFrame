# Riot Dice SRD

**One of only two genuinely, verifiably openly-licensed wargame texts found in this entire survey.** CC BY 4.0, commercial use permitted.

## Identity

- **Author:** Mike Hutchinson / Planet Smasher Games (also [[gaslands-refuelled]], [[space-gits]])
- **URL:** https://planetsmashergames.com/riot-dice/
- **What it is:** the dice engine extracted from [[space-gits]] and published as a standalone System Reference Document.

## Licensing — CC BY 4.0, confirmed from the rights holder

**Confirmed.** Quoted directly from the publisher's own page:

> "I offer the text of my Riot Dice System Reference Document ('SRD') under a Creative Commons CC by 4.0 licence. This allows you to remix, adapt, distribute and build upon in any medium or format, so long as you give attribution to me as the creator. The licence allows for commercial use."

This is the real thing, and it clears every bar that [[free-as-in-beer-vs-openly-licensed]] sets:

- ✅ Grant is from the **rights holder**, on **their own site**, in **their own words**
- ✅ **Irrevocable** (CC 4.0 licences are)
- ✅ **Redistribution** of the text permitted
- ✅ **Derivative works** permitted
- ✅ **Commercial use** permitted
- ✅ Only obligation is **attribution**

**We could ship this content.** With attribution to Mike Hutchinson.

## Scope — read this carefully

The grant covers **the SRD text**, i.e. the dice engine. It does **not** cover [[space-gits]] itself (a paid product), its setting, art, or unit content. "Riot Dice is open" ≠ "Space Gits is open."

## The strategic reading

This is the **"open engine, closed content"** pattern: a designer keeps the game commercial while opening the mechanical core so others can build on it. That's worth noticing for two reasons:

1. It's a **template for how to ask** other indie designers ([[turnip28]] especially) for terms — asking for an SRD-style grant over mechanics is a much smaller ask than "may I ship your game," and there's now precedent.
2. It suggests the honest architecture for most candidates: **the module ships an engine; the user supplies the data.** Riot Dice is a rare case where a chunk of the *content* can come along too.

## Activation model

**Partial / unverified.** Dexterity-based: dice are physically thrown into the play area, models move toward where they land, damage tracked via dice "towers" ([Wargamer](https://www.wargamer.com/space-gits/indie-game-for-warhammer-40k-orks)). **Not confirmed against the SRD text itself,** and the turn/activation sequence was not described by any source found.

> [!warning] Openly licensed but probably not implementable
> The cruel irony: the most permissively licensed text found is for a game whose core resolution is *throwing physical dice onto a table and measuring where they landed*. There is no faithful VTT representation of that. **Open licence, wrong game.** See [[space-gits]] for this as a scope boundary.

## Suitability

**Not an implementation target** — the dexterity core doesn't map to a VTT. **High value as a licensing precedent** and as proof that the open-SRD ask is a real, answerable one in this hobby.
