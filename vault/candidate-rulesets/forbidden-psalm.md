# Forbidden Psalm

Rules-light grimdark skirmish game built on Mörk Borg. **The strongest overall early candidate**: mechanics are legally reusable, rules are light, and the activation model is simple enough to build on.

## Identity

- **Author:** Kevin Rahman (credited "Will RD" on the original Kickstarter)
- **URL:** https://www.forbiddenpsalm.com/forbidden-psalm
- **Lineage:** an unofficial Mörk Borg miniatures game

## Licensing — published under the Mörk Borg Third Party License

**Confirmed.** Quoted from the official page:

> "Forbidden Psalm is an independent production by Kevin Rahman and is not affiliated with Ockult Örtmästare Games or Stockholm Kartell. It is published under the MÖRK BORG Third Party License."

That license — see [[mork-borg-third-party-license]] for the full fetched text — grants, critically:

> "The mechanics and game rules of MÖRK BORG may be reused and referenced freely."

...royalty-free, commercial use allowed, **no approval needed**.

### The important nuance, stated carefully

The Mörk Borg license governs **Mörk Borg's** mechanics. Forbidden Psalm is a *third-party product built under* that license — which does **not** automatically place *Forbidden Psalm's own* original rules under the same terms. Rahman authored original skirmish mechanics on top of Mörk Borg's chassis, and **his** rights in **his** additions were not established by this research.

> [!warning] Do not over-read this
> "Forbidden Psalm is under the Mörk Borg TPL" means *Rahman had permission to build it*. It does **not** by itself mean *we have permission to reimplement Forbidden Psalm*. Whether Rahman extends comparable terms downstream is **not found** and needs either (a) a licence statement in his product, or (b) a direct ask. He has clearly opted into an open-ish ecosystem, so the ask is very likely to land — but it is an ask, not an assumption. **Human legal review required.**

The product itself is sold commercially (price not found); a **free quickstart** exists ("Forbidden Psalm: The Last War quick start").

## Activation model — alternating single-model, move-then-act

**Partial** (from a detailed [rules review](http://shaggywargaming.blogspot.com/2021/06/review-forbidden-psalm-by-k-r-d-designs.html); rulebook not fetched directly):

- Players **alternate activating a single model**.
- Each activation: one **movement** and one **action** — but **must move before acting, or the activation ends**. A neat ordering constraint.
- Move distance = **5 + Agility** inches.
- Actions: ranged/melee attack, use equipment, cast spell/Omen, interact.
- **Monsters activate as a group** after all player models have gone, with a die roll randomising order among them.
- Close combat has a **counterattack risk** for the defender (−3 modifier roll).

Close to the presumed engine default (alternating single activation), with two mild wrinkles: the move-before-act ordering rule, and monsters-as-a-batch-after-players (a mild echo of [[solo-and-ai-driven-activation-as-an-engine-requirement]]).

## Complexity

**Light.** Rules-light, random-table character creation (Darkest Dungeon-ish), narrative/roguelike focus, small warbands, 2'x2' table.

## Suitability

**The best-balanced early target found.** Reasoning:

- Licensing is the most tractable in the survey — an open ecosystem, a real license, a designer plausibly amenable to a downstream ask.
- Complexity is low — a realistic first module, unlike [[necromunda-community-edition]] or [[five-parsecs-from-home]].
- Activation is near-default — it validates the happy path without immediately demanding an exotic scheduler.
- Small table, small warbands — a good fit for a VTT.

Compare [[danger-close]], which has a *cleaner* licence (CC BY-SA, no ask needed) but wholly unknown mechanics. **Forbidden Psalm is the better-understood bet; Danger Close is the cleaner-licensed one.** Both are worth pursuing.
