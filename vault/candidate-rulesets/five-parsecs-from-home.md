# Five Parsecs from Home (3rd ed)

Solo sci-fi skirmish campaign game. **Paid, all rights reserved** — the "verify, don't assume" instruction was warranted, and the answer is no.

## Identity

- **Author:** Ivan Sorensen (Nordic Weasel Games), retained as lead writer
- **Publisher:** **Modiphius Entertainment** (since 2023)
- **URL:** https://modiphius.net/en-us/products/five-parsecs-from-home

## Licensing — paid commercial, no open licence

**Confirmed.** Modiphius' own product page lists the physical hardback at **$48.11**, "Includes free PDF on purchase" — i.e. the PDF is **bundled with a paid purchase, not separately free**. No Creative Commons, OGL, or ORC language anywhere on the page. **Cannot ship content.**

> [!note] "Free PDF on purchase" is a bundling term, not a licence
> This phrasing reliably generates false positives in searches for free rules. It means "you get the PDF too when you buy the book." It grants nothing.

## Ownership history — matters for who you'd even ask

**Confirmed.** Originally an independent Nordic Weasel Games title; **Modiphius acquired the IP in June 2023**, specifically: *"Modiphius purchased the rights to Five Parsecs From Home, Five Leagues From the Borderlands, Weaseltech and the 'Fivecore' series... including Five Men in Normandy and Five Men at Kursk."* Sorensen stayed on as lead writer.

Sources: [Nordic Weasel FAQ](https://nordicweasel.posthaven.com/faq-on-nordic-weasel-games-and-modiphius), [Modiphius announcement](https://modiphius.net/blogs/news/modiphius-entertainment-acquires-five-parsecs-from-home-five-leagues-from-the-borderlands), [ICv2](https://icv2.com/articles/news/view/54367/modiphius-acquires-nordic-weasel-games-ips).

> [!warning] The acquisition closes the permission route
> This is the single most consequential fact about this title. An indie designer might have said yes to a fan module. **Modiphius is a publisher with a licensing department**, and licensed digital products are a revenue line they sell — not give. Sorensen *cannot* grant permission; he no longer owns it.
>
> Generalisable lesson: **check current ownership before planning a permission ask.** A friendly indie designer's name on the cover does not mean an indie rights holder.

## Activation model — reaction-roll partial activation

**Partial** (two independent reviews, consistent; not the rulebook):

- Solo/co-op game. Enemies run an **"AI"** — priority-based behaviour **stances (Attack / Defend / Patrol)** rather than a fixed flowchart, requiring some player interpretation.
- In the *Tactics* supplement: sides alternate taking a "phase." The active side activates **one unit normally**, then **rolls 3d6 to activate additional units whose Reaction stat is ≥ one of the dice**.

Sources: [hws3 review](https://hws3.wordpress.com/2022/11/22/review-five-parsecs-from-home-3rd-edition/), [The Tabletop Skirmisher](https://www.thetabletopskirmisher.com/2021/05/the-5-parsecs-chain-reaction-2015-hack.html).

> [!tip] Two distinct engine demands
> 1. **Reaction-gated partial activation:** how many of your units act each phase is *rolled for*, not fixed. Neither "all units act" nor "one unit acts."
> 2. **Interpretive AI:** unlike [[rangers-of-shadow-deep]]'s deterministic algorithm, stance-based priorities *require player judgement*. That's a meaningfully harder automation target — the engine can prompt and assist but **cannot fully resolve** it. A useful boundary on how far solo automation can go. See [[solo-and-ai-driven-activation-as-an-engine-requirement]].

## Complexity

**Medium-heavy.** A full campaign game — crew management, world steps, loot, injuries — not just a battle engine.

## Suitability

**No route.** Paid, corporately owned, campaign-heavy. Retained for the reaction-roll activation and the interpretive-AI boundary.
