# Fan-content policies are not open licenses

A recurring trap in this survey. Several publishers offer a "fan content policy" that reads generously and grants almost nothing useful.

## What they typically say

From Planet Smasher Games' [Fan Content Policy](https://planetsmashergames.com/fan-content-policy/) (covering [[gaslands-refuelled]]) — representative of the pattern:

> "You cannot monetise your content" — "As soon as you monetise your fan content (by selling it, adding a tip jar, patreon subscription, ad-funding, etc.) it ceases to be fan content."

> "Your content cannot be reasonably mistaken for an 'official' product."

Plus: no reprinting extensive rulebook text, and mandatory disclaimer boilerplate.

[[trench-crusade]]'s community license follows the same shape, adding an explicit bar on using "any part of the rules" to create other games, and a broad grant-back of rights in *your* work to the publisher.

## Why they don't help

| Open license (CC/OGL/ORC) | Fan content policy |
|---|---|
| Irrevocable | **Revocable at will** |
| Permits redistribution of the licensed text | **Usually forbids reproducing rules text** — the exact thing a module needs |
| Commercial use generally permitted | **Non-commercial only** |
| Standing grant, no permission needed | Often requires application/approval |
| Sub-licensable / transferable | Personal, non-transferable |

The revocability point deserves weight. Building a module on a revocable permission means the publisher can end the project at any time, for any reason — including a change of ownership or a licensing deal that makes your module inconvenient. That is a poor foundation regardless of how friendly the current terms look.

## The distinction that matters for a module

A Foundry module needs to embed **rules logic and stat data**. Fan-content policies are written for battle reports, painting blogs, homebrew warbands, and STLs. They are essentially never written with software in mind, and the "don't reproduce the rules text" clause is aimed squarely at what a module does.

## Practical stance

A fan-content policy may support shipping an **engine with no data** (mechanics implemented in code, user enters their own stats). It does not support shipping content. Where a policy seems to permit more, that is a question for a human lawyer, not an inference.
