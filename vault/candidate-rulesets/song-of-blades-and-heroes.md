# Song of Blades and Heroes

The famous **roll 1–3 dice, two failures = turnover** activation engine. Paid and unlicensed — but mechanically it's the sharpest test case in the survey alongside [[rampant-system]].

## Identity

- **Author:** Andrea Sfiligoi
- **Publisher:** Ganesha Games
- **URL:** https://www.ganeshagames.net/product_info.php?products_id=7

## Licensing — commercial, no open licence found

**Confirmed (as to absence).** Commercial PDF, **$8.00** per the live product page. No open/free licence found for the core rules text, on Ganesha's site or elsewhere.

> [!note] The derivative-games puzzle
> Multiple commercially published third-party games use the SBH activation engine as standalone products — Shadowsea, DeepWars, Song of Our Ancestors, Firebrand. But **no public licence document from Ganesha Games governs this** — unlike [[mork-borg-third-party-license]] or [[mausritter-srd]], there is no licence page.
>
> This looks like **informal or privately negotiated permission**, not a public grant. **Partial** — the derivative products are confirmed to exist; the legal mechanism is **unverified**.
>
> Do not reason "others did it, so we can." A private deal between Sfiligoi and a publisher confers nothing on us. If SBH is wanted, the path is **asking Ganesha directly**, not inferring a licence from third-party products. There's an encouraging signal here (Sfiligoi evidently says yes to people) but a signal is not a licence.

Also worth noting for the mechanic specifically: **game mechanics are not copyrightable** in most jurisdictions — only their *expression* is. This is a real principle and it's why so many SBH-alikes exist. But it is a **question for a lawyer, not a research note**, and it does not license the rules *text*, stat lines, or army data. Do not lean on it without advice.

## Activation model — the risk/reward turnover

**Confirmed** ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Song_of_Blades_and_Heroes), rules PDF text):

Each model has a **Quality (Q)** stat — a d6 target number (lower is better; Q3+ means 3 or higher succeeds).

- Nominate **one model** to activate, then **choose to roll 1, 2, or 3 dice**.
- Each die meeting/beating Q = **one action**.
- **Roll two failures in that activation → the model's activation ends AND the turn immediately passes to the opponent** — the "turnover."

The tension is exact and elegant: more dice = more actions, but a sharply higher chance of handing your opponent the initiative mid-turn.

> [!tip] Architecturally
> Like [[rampant-system]], **turn length is non-deterministic and terminated by a die roll** — but SBH goes further: the *player* chooses their risk exposure per activation. The engine must model an activation as a *variable-length, player-tuned gamble* whose failure ends the entire turn.
>
> The convergence with Rampant (two unrelated designers, same "greed ends your turn" idea) suggests this is a **design family**, not a one-off. An engine that hosts both has probably solved turn-scheduling properly. See [[activation-model-comparison]].

## Complexity

**Very light.** "Learn in one game," d6 only, minimal bookkeeping. Widely cited as one of the lightest skirmish systems in the hobby — and the benchmark reviewers compare [[open-combat]] and [[this-is-not-a-test]] against.

## Suitability

**Not shippable without permission.** But: light rules + a famous, distinctive, well-documented mechanic + a designer with a track record of licensing to third parties = **the best candidate for a permission request** after [[forbidden-psalm]]. Worth an email.
