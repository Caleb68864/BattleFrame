# Free-as-in-beer vs openly licensed

The single distinction that decides what a Foundry module may contain. Conflating these two is the most expensive mistake available in this project.

## The distinction

**Free-as-in-beer**: the publisher gives away the PDF at no cost. Copyright is fully retained. You may read it. You may not copy it, redistribute it, or embed its text and stat data in software. Cost of acquisition says **nothing** about redistribution rights.

**Openly licensed**: the rights holder has granted a standing, irrevocable licence (CC-BY, CC-BY-SA, OGL 1.0a, ORC, public domain / CC0) permitting copying and redistribution, usually including derivative works, under stated conditions. Only this permits shipping content.

## Why it matters here

The project's entire premise is that free rules let a module **ship the actual rules content and army data** instead of requiring users to own a rulebook. That premise only holds for the *second* category. A free-as-in-beer game gives you exactly the same legal position as a $50 hardback: design against it, ship nothing.

Most "free rules" wargames turn out to be free-as-in-beer. [[trench-crusade]] is the sharpest example — famously free forever, and its licence *specifically* forbids using the rules in other projects.

## Signals that do NOT establish an open licence

Each of these was encountered during this survey and each is worthless as evidence:

- **A free download button.** Says nothing about rights. ([[trench-crusade]], [[turnip28]])
- **A licence tag on archive.org / scribd / pdfcoffee.** Applied by *uploaders*, not rights holders. The Internet Archive copy of Turnip28 is tagged CC BY-NC by a random user — legally meaningless. See [[turnip28]].
- **A fan-content policy.** Permission to make fan works ≠ a redistribution grant, and these are typically non-commercial and revocable. See [[fan-content-policies-are-not-open-licenses]].
- **An expired promotional giveaway.** [[frostgrave]]'s 2020 free-PDF promo code was a sale, not a licence.
- **A "compatible with X" third-party program.** Usually excludes rules text, requires application/approval, and is revocable. See [[trench-crusade]].
- **Community wikis reproducing the rules.** Their existence is evidence of lax enforcement, not of permission.
- **Memory / "I'm pretty sure it's CC".** Never acceptable. Fetch the licence page and cite it.

## The operating rule

> Assume all-rights-reserved until a licence page from the **rights holder** says otherwise, in writing, and you have read it and cited its URL. "Not found" is a valid and honest finding. "Probably CC" is not.

If a candidate is only free-as-in-beer, that is not automatically fatal — it means the module ships *mechanics* (an engine implementing the rules) and the *user* supplies the data. But it forfeits the advantage that motivated looking at free games at all, so it should change the ranking.
