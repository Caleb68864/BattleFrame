# There Are No Stated Attribution Requirements — Because There Is No Licence to Attribute Under

Direct answer to "what are the attribution requirements?" — **None are stated. This is not good news.**

Attribution requirements are a feature of **permissive licences**: CC BY tells you exactly how to credit because it is *granting* you something in exchange. OPR grants nothing ([[licence-opr-rules-have-no-open-licence]]), so it specifies no attribution terms. **The absence of an attribution clause is the absence of a licence, not a waiver of one.**

## What the documents actually carry

The v3.5.1 Core Rules footer, on every page, verbatim:

> Copyright © OPR Games. All Rights Reserved   |   Created By: Gaetano Ferrara   |   www.onepagerules.com

The Beginner's Guide v3.5.1:

> Copyright © 2024 OPR Games - First Published 2024

These are **copyright assertions and a credit line**, not licence terms. Nothing on the site converts them into "credit us and you may redistribute."

## The trap to avoid

The tempting move — "I'll just add a prominent credit and a 'not affiliated with OPR' disclaimer, and that makes it fine" — **does not work**:

- Attribution does not cure infringement. Crediting the author of a work you had no right to copy leaves you having copied it.
- A disclaimer of affiliation addresses **trademark confusion**, not **copyright**. It is necessary but nowhere near sufficient.

## What attribution *is* good for

Once a module ships legitimately — i.e. as [[design-ship-a-rules-engine-with-no-bundled-opr-content]], or under permission from [[licence-ask-opr-directly-is-the-only-clean-path]] — you should still:

- Credit OPR Games and Gaetano Ferrara, and link `onepagerules.com`.
- State clearly that the module is unofficial and unaffiliated, and that users must obtain the rules from OPR.
- Avoid using OPR logos or game titles as the module's branding (trademark).

Do that because it is right and reduces trademark exposure — **not** because it licenses anything.
