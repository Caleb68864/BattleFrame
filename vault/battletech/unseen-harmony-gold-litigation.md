# The Unseen / Harmony Gold — Proof This IP Estate Litigates

Context for why BattleTech demands more legal caution than a typical indie ruleset. This is not hypothetical risk; it has repeatedly gone to court.

## The origin

FASA licensed mecha designs from Japanese anime (**Macross, Dougram, Crusher Joe**) in 1984 via intermediary Twentieth Century Imports. By 1994 FASA discovered the intermediary **may have lacked authority to grant those rights**. A 1997 out-of-court settlement required FASA to stop using the images.

The affected designs — the **"Unseen"** — include some of the most iconic 'Mechs: **Warhammer, Marauder, Archer, Rifleman, Crusader, Thunderbolt, Wolverine, Griffin, Shadow Hawk**, and 50+ others.

## The litigation timeline

| Year | Event |
|---|---|
| **2009** | Harmony Gold (Robotech owner) issues cease & desist over MechWarrior preview materials, claiming Macross-derived designs (e.g. Warhammer) infringe |
| **2017** | Harmony Gold **sues Catalyst Game Labs, Piranha Games, and Harebrained Schemes** over redesigned Unseen 'Mechs |
| **2019** | Piranha refused to back down; case **dismissed with prejudice**, undisclosed settlement allowing all three to continue using redesigned versions |

## The load-bearing distinction: ART vs STATS

**This is the useful takeaway for the engine.** The settlement restricted **artwork**:

> The game statistics and unit designations remained usable — only the original visual designs required replacement.

That's why the 2015 "retcon" worked: Catalyst commissioned **new artwork** while keeping identical stats, names, and canon.

## What this means for a Foundry module

1. **Art is the sharpest hazard.** A VTT module is art-hungry (tokens, portraits, unit cards). Shipping 'Mech artwork — *especially* Unseen designs — is the fastest route to a legal problem. Any module should ship **no BattleTech art**.
2. **Stats sit in a different, less-settled bucket** than art. That's directionally encouraging for a rules engine — but note the "stats remained usable" finding is about *Catalyst's* rights vs *Harmony Gold*, **not** about a third party's right to copy Catalyst's stats. **Do not over-read it.**
3. **This estate sues.** Topps/Catalyst/Harmony Gold have all been litigants. Casual risk-taking is inappropriate here.
