# Free-to-Read Is Not Free-to-Ship (the core BattleTech licensing trap)

The most dangerous available error in this research would be reasoning:

> "The Alpha Strike quick-start rules are free → therefore a Foundry module can ship Alpha Strike content."

**That inference does not hold.** These are two unrelated things:

| Question | BattleTech answer |
|---|---|
| Can I download the rules for $0? | **Yes** — confirmed, [[alpha-strike-quick-start-is-free]] |
| Can I redistribute / reimplement them in software? | **No grant exists** — see below |

## Why the second answer is "no grant exists"

Searched for and **did not find**:
- any BattleTech SRD
- any Open Game Licence / Creative Commons grant on any Catalyst rules PDF
- any published Catalyst fan-content licence — see [[catalyst-fan-content-policy-not-found]]

Catalyst's word for these PDFs is "free downloadable **products**." That is pricing language. Every one of them remains fully copyrighted, with Topps trademarks asserted ([[topps-owns-battletech-ip]]).

## The one real nuance in the module's favour

**Game mechanics are not copyrightable in the US** — only their *expression* is. This is longstanding doctrine (*Baker v. Selden*; and games specifically, e.g. *Allen v. Academic Games League of America*). So an engine could plausibly implement *rules logic* while shipping **none** of Catalyst's text, tables, art, names, or unit stats.

That is precisely how [[megamek-what-it-is]] has survived for 20+ years.

⚠️ **This paragraph is my structural understanding, not legal advice.** The line between "uncopyrightable mechanic" and "infringing expression" is genuinely fact-specific, and stat blocks/tables sit uncomfortably near it. This needs **human legal review** before any content ships. See [[licensing-verdict-battletech]].
