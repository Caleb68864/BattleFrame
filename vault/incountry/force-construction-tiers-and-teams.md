# Force Construction — Pick a Force and Three Units; No Points System

INCOUNTRY explicitly **rejects points-based list building.**

**Confirmed:** "you simply **choose a force and pick three units**, rather than point-counting and force-building." Teams are represented by **cards listing each model's weapons, skills, and roles** — so the unit card is the stat container.

Published forces (from the storefront collections, https://inxcountry.com/): **Blackthorn, Black Shell, Direwolf, Lion, Tiger Shark**, plus **Vehicles** and a **Legacy Pod**. Whether these are mechanically distinct factions or cosmetic ranges is **not found** — the marketing does not say, and no source found compares faction rules.

## Tier system (RECON — attribution uncertain)

The **INCOUNTRY: RECON Forces & Scenarios** guide (https://www.scribd.com/document/662772313/INX-RECON-FORCES-SCENARIOS) describes assembling squads by **"choosing a tier and teams,"** with **tier 1–3 force lists** and a distinction between **"primary and proxy teams."**

**Confidence caveat:** this is sourced from a **RECON** document, and the Scribd page exposed only metadata, not the document body. Whether tiers exist in **INX 2.0** is **unverified** — see [[editions-inx-recon-vs-incountry-2-0]]. Do not assume the tier system is the 2.0 force construction model.

## Why this matters architecturally

No points means **no validation arithmetic** — force legality is a structural check ("one force, three units, from this tier") rather than a budget sum. That is a materially simpler roster validator than most wargames need.
